<objective>
Write down what this phase decided and what it measured, and correct the one superseded
inference Phase 10 left in an ops record.

Purpose: SC-2 is not satisfied by a correct `difficultyForWave` — it explicitly requires the
ramp be "written down rather than tuned by feel in code". And `BOARD-GENERATOR.md` § Limits
currently states that boards at the clear-time tail are *plausibly unfinishable by a real
player*. Research disproved the premise behind that inference with a controlled experiment,
and a superseded inference sitting in an ops record is precisely what that section exists to
prevent.

Output: `docs/ops/ENDLESS-MODE.md`, an amended `docs/ops/BOARD-GENERATOR.md`, and the device
SC-5 reading or a dated open assumption in its place.

## What the documents must NOT say

**Do not re-tune `SCHEDULE`, and do not write anything that invites a re-tune.** Research
settled the open question with measurement, and the answer was that the premise was wrong:

- A 500-seed scan at difficulty 20 specifically found **0 non-wins** (p50 172.5 s, p95 446.2 s,
  worst 1495.3 s). There is no unclearable board.
- **The tail is a trajectory property, not a board property.** Replaying the 8 slowest d=20
  boards across 7 paddle offsets collapsed `s=33` from 1495.3 s to 83.0 s — an 18× spread on
  the identical lattice. Changing only the gameplay RNG seed did the same.
- Per-difficulty maxima are **non-monotone** in `d`: d=17 peaks at 2735.3 s, worse than d=20,
  so "the top of the range is the hard part" is also untrue.
- A re-tune buys roughly 34 % off the median and **re-rolls the tail rather than removing it**,
  at the cost of Phase 10's 21 000-board sweep, its monotonicity proof, and the freshly
  discharged A1 device record.

**No softlock exists:** 1 900 board plays with zero non-wins, plus the reachability Theorem,
plus `applyLivesFromBallCount` untouched. A player who cannot clear wave 21 loses by missing,
which is the intended ending condition.
</objective>
