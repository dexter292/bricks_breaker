**The policy itself**, one sentence, set off so it cannot be missed: a date is playable if and
only if it has no stored result. Then one sub-section per decision, each stating the case and what
the rule produces without adding a second rule:

- *Backwards* (D-02): a date that already has a result is read-only. Opening it shows the stored
  result; it cannot be replayed. Winding the clock back gains nothing.
- *Forwards* (D-03): playable, and the streak breaks by itself, because the intervening dates have
  no stored result. There is no anti-cheat branch anywhere in the code, and that absence is the
  design rather than an omission.
- *Timezone travel* (D-04): not distinguished from clock tampering. Offline and without a trusted
  time source the two are not distinguishable, so any special case would be a guess dressed as a
  policy.
- *No stored watermark* (D-05): state what was rejected and why — a highest-date-ever-seen field is
  a second thing that can corrupt independently, and the criterion's whole point is deriving state
  from stored dates rather than from a separate counter.

**"Measured behaviour"**, copying `ENDLESS-MODE.md:135`: the executed evidence, with its
provenance. The five-locale measurement that rules out a locale-derived key; the two real 2026 DST
days and what the calendar-arithmetic form produced on each; why a fixed-day-in-milliseconds
boundary skips a date on the short day and repeats one on the long day; and the 731-key diffusion
replay showing no collision and no adjacent-date correlation. State for each whether it was
executed under the engine this app ships, under Node, or both — the endless doc's habit of
labelling its evidence is what makes it trustworthy.

**The two discretionary numbers**, with the reasoning as shipped: the fixed difficulty and its
position on the generator's published table between the tutorial board and the showpiece, and the
history window with its byte arithmetic and the reason the number is what it is rather than a
rounder one. State the shipped caveat that the generator's own sweep recorded no unwinnable board
at that difficulty, which is what makes "the streak counts dates played, not dates won" safe.

**"Accepted costs"** — three named, bounded, deliberately unmitigated things, each written as a
decision a later reader will find rather than a defect they will file:

- *The practice hole* (D-08): because the board is derived from the date, exiting before the final
  life and re-entering returns the same board, so a determined player can rehearse. There is no
  re-roll — the board never changes — only rehearsal. Closing it would mean making an abandoned run
  close the date, which costs every interrupted player their day.
- *The read-failure second attempt*: treating an unreadable record as "no stored result" means a
  transient storage error can hand a player a second attempt at the day. The alternative — treating
  unreadable as closed — locks a player out of their day on a transient fault, which is strictly
  worse.
- *Blob tampering*: accepted by design. There is no server to validate against and no leaderboard
  to protect; an offline single-player streak has no adversary but the player.

**"Limits"**, copying `ENDLESS-MODE.md:385`: what is not known. The engine resolves the device
time zone once per runtime and caches it for that runtime's lifetime, so a device timezone change
is not observed until the app is relaunched; D-01 covers the outcome, because whichever local date
the app derives, it is playable if and only if it has no stored result. State that the desktop
harness proved the cache is the engine's own rather than the C library's, that a real OS timezone
change on a device is a different mechanism and was not observable, and that if a device check
shows the date does re-derive without a relaunch, this paragraph should be **narrowed, not
deleted**. Also state that the Android engine slice was never executed, and that the automated
suite runs on a different JavaScript engine from the one the app ships — a green suite is evidence
about that other engine, and the engine evidence in this document comes from executing it directly.

unverified.
